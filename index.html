Teach your webcam to recognize thumbs up and thumbs down

Collect photos, turn them into black-and-white silhouettes, train a classifier, and watch its predictions on live camera images. Everything runs in this browser tab. Nothing is installed and nothing is uploaded.

camera → background → labeled photos → grayscale → black-and-white mask → cleaned silhouette → features → training → testing → live predictions

Before you start

  • Use a plain, still background that contrasts with your hand. Keep the camera and lighting still the whole time.
  • Do the steps in order. Each step shows its result. "Show the code" shows the exact code that produced it.
  • No webcam, or it's blocked? Click Use practice camera. It shows a drawn hand you control with the empty / up / down buttons in the top bar.
  • Photos are kept in this tab only. Refreshing or closing it erases them. Use step 11 to download a copy.
01

Open your webcam

Click Start camera and allow your browser to use the webcam. The white square is the region of interest (ROI). Only what is inside it is used: it is cropped, mirrored to match the preview, and shrunk to 224 × 224 pixels.

  • Keep your whole hand and thumb inside the square, with as little forearm as practical.
  • Keep your face and body outside the square.
  • Avoid strong shadows.

Capture a photo to see the cropped image and its size.

02
Record an empty background

      
Take your hand out of the square and click Capture background. Seven frames are recorded and combined into one reference image using the median of every pixel, which is the middle value of the seven. That ignores small flickers and camera noise.

      
Later, pixels that differ from this reference become the foreground. Keep the camera and lighting still. If either one changes, record the background again.

04
Convert a photo to grayscale

      
Pick one training photo to follow through steps 4 to 7. Click a different one at any time.

      

      
Collect some training photos in step 3 to fill in steps 4 to 7.

      

        
        
        
      

      
Point at the original or grayscale photo to inspect a pixel.

      
A color pixel has three numbers (red, green, blue). A grayscale pixel has one brightness number: gray = 0.299 × red + 0.587 × green + 0.114 × blue. Green counts most because our eyes are most sensitive to it. Grayscale still has 256 levels; the next step reduces it to black and white.

05
Create and clean a black-and-white mask

      
The sliders in this step change how every photo is processed, including the live mask previews in steps 3 and 9. 


      
A. Blur, then subtract the background

      
A small Gaussian blur reduces pixel noise. Then each pixel is compared with the same pixel in the background. The absolute difference is large where something changed (brighter or darker) and near zero where nothing did.

      
      

        
        
        
      

      
Show the code


      
B. Threshold the difference

      
Each pixel whose difference is above the threshold becomes white (255). Everything else becomes black (0). The histogram counts how many pixels have each difference value; the ones right of the line turn white.

      
      
      

        
        
      

      

      
If the hand disappears, try a smaller threshold or a background with more contrast. If much of the background turns white, try a larger threshold or record a better background. Choose this using training photos, before the final test.

      
Show the code


      
C. Remove specks and fill small gaps

      
Opening (erode, then dilate) removes small white specks. Closing (dilate, then erode) fills small black gaps. Both slide a small round shape called a kernel over the mask. A kernel that is too big can erase useful details such as the thumb.

06
Isolate the hand and normalize its size

      
Keep only the largest connected white region. In a controlled scene it should be the hand, but this step does not understand anatomy: a moving object or a large shadow can also become the largest region. Check the result.

      
Then crop around that region and center it in a 64 × 64 square, keeping its proportions. It is never rotated or flipped, because thumb direction is the label being learned.

      

        
        
        
      

      

      
The same steps for every image

      
The preprocess function below packages steps 4 to 6. It also rejects a photo when the hand covers less than 2% of the square, or when more than 75% of the square changed. An empty or broken mask should not be sent to the classifier as if it were a hand. The same function is used for training, testing and live predictions, and its settings are saved with the trained model.

07
Turn the silhouette into numbers

      
The classifier needs a list of numbers, not a picture. Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG) describes the directions of edges in different parts of the image. Where the edges are, and which way they point, separates an upward thumb from a downward one.

      
The silhouette is split into 8 × 8 pixel cells. Each cell sorts its edges into 9 direction bins. Neighbouring cells are grouped into overlapping 16 × 16 pixel blocks and normalized.

08
Build the dataset and train a classifier

      
Every training photo goes through the same pipeline, each with its own background reference. Photos with a rejected mask are counted and shown. If many are rejected, fix the masking in step 5. Adding more unusable photos will not help.

      
The image processing and HOG steps use fixed rules. The logistic regression classifier is the part that learns from your labeled examples. Despite its name it is a classification model: it learns one weight per feature, adds up weight × feature, and turns the total into a probability. Before that, every feature is standardized (shifted and scaled to mean 0 and spread 1) using training photos only. Nothing is pretrained; the model starts at zero and learns only from your photos.

      
The processing settings changed after training. Testing and live predictions still use the settings saved with the model. Train again to use the new ones.

Training steps

        
Each step adjusts all 1,765 weights to lower the loss, a measure of how wrong the model's probabilities are on the training photos (lower is better). This lab uses Newton's method, which usually finishes in about ten steps.

    --
    Investigate a failure
    
      
    Try a different hand position, a greater distance, or another person. Decide whether the problem starts with the mask or with classifying a reasonable silhouette.
    
      
      
        
    • Masking problem: the hand disappears, shadows dominate, or the scene turns white. Check the background, lighting and threshold.
      • 
        
    • Classification problem: the silhouette looks reasonable but the label is wrong. Add varied, correctly labeled examples and train again.
      • 
        
    • Unknown gesture: an open palm or another object is assigned one of the two learned labels. A further project can add and evaluate more categories.
      • 
      
    
      
    After using a test to make changes, collect a new final test. Report sample counts with your results. A useful demonstration includes a success, a failure, and an explanation of an improvement.
    
      
    Troubleshooting
    
      
    
        
    Permission denied
    
        
    Click the camera icon in the address bar, allow the camera for this site, then click Start camera again. Also check the operating system's camera permission for the browser. If a school policy blocks it, use the practice camera.
    
        
    Camera busy or blank
    
        
    Close other apps or tabs using the webcam (video calls, other camera sites) and click Start camera again.
    
        
    Mask mostly white
    
        
    The camera moved or the lighting changed. Keep the camera still and record the background again.
    
        
    Mask loses the thumb
    
        
    Improve contrast with the background, lower the threshold slightly, or try a smaller kernel. Check training photos before the final test.
    
        
    New settings do not affect live predictions
    
        
    Train again in step 8. Testing and live predictions use the settings saved with the model.
    
        
    The page was refreshed and everything is gone
    
        
    Photos only exist in the open tab. Load a saved ZIP in step 11, or collect new photos.
